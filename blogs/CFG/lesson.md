# The Seduction of Success

## Lesson profile

- Source: *Counterfeit Gods*, chapter 4
- Primary passage: 2 Kings 5:1–16
- Supporting passages: Genesis 1:26–28; 1 Corinthians 4:7; James 1:17; Isaiah 53
- Main idea: Success is a good gift, but it becomes an idol when we ask it to establish our worth, secure our future, or earn God's favor.
- Gospel connection: God's grace cannot be achieved or purchased. Jesus, the true Suffering Servant, has completed the saving work we could never do.
- Suggested duration: 60 minutes

## Flow

1. Opening question — 8 minutes
2. The biblical goodness of work — 7 minutes
3. Read and explore Naaman's story — 15 minutes
4. Diagnose the idol of success — 10 minutes
5. Barter versus grace — 10 minutes
6. Small-group discussion — 15 minutes
7. Response and prayer — 5 minutes

## Leader emphasis

The lesson should not shame ambition, excellence, or hard work. Keep returning to the distinction between receiving achievement as a gift and using it as a verdict on our worth. Naaman's resistance exposes the pride that would rather accomplish a difficult task than admit need and receive grace.

The unnamed servant girl is an important bridge to the gospel. Though she has little social power, she points her enemy toward healing. Jesus is the greater Suffering Servant: he bears the cost of forgiveness and gives believers an identity that is received rather than achieved.

## Discussion questions

1. What lies beneath your desire to succeed or to be seen as successful?
2. When does accomplishment stop being something you do and become the measure of your worth?
3. Where do you slip into a barter relationship with God: "If I do this, God should do that"?
4. What does Naaman need to surrender before he can receive grace?
5. How does Jesus' completed work free you to pursue excellence without being enslaved by it?

## Weekly practice

- Notice one situation where performance is controlling your peace.
- Thank God for one ability or opportunity as a gift rather than a possession.
- Do one faithful act that may never be noticed or praised.
