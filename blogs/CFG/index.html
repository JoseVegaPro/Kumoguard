Chapter 4 · Small Group Guide

The Seduction
of Success

Success is a good gift. It becomes a counterfeit god when we ask it to prove our worth, secure our future, or make us acceptable.

2 Kings 5:1–16 60 minutes
Begin the conversation ↓
Open Story Diagnose Grace Discuss
01

Opening · 8 minutes

What does success promise us?

When you imagine a “successful person,” what do you picture—and what do you assume that person feels inside?

Big idea

Achievement can describe what we have done. It cannot tell us who we are or what we are worth.

02

Biblical foundation · 7 minutes

A good thing becomes a god thing

Genesis presents fruitful work, cultivation, and responsibility as part of humanity’s calling. The problem is not achievement itself. The problem begins when achievement becomes the source of our identity and security.

Genesis 1:26–28

God gives humanity the responsibility to cultivate, multiply, and exercise wise stewardship over creation.

Gift “I use my abilities faithfully.”
Idol “My abilities prove my value.”
03

Read together · 15 minutes

Naaman had everything—
except what he needed

Read 2 Kings 5:1–16 aloud. Ask different people to read the narrator, servant girl, king, Elisha’s messenger, Naaman, and Naaman’s servants.

1

Accomplished, but powerless

Naaman has rank, reputation, courage, and influence. None of them can heal him.

2

Looking to power

He arrives with a royal letter, wealth, and status—tools that have worked everywhere else.

3

Offended by simplicity

He expects ceremony or a great task. Instead, he receives a simple instruction he cannot turn into an achievement.

4

Healed by receiving

Naaman humbles himself, washes, and discovers that God’s gift cannot be purchased or earned.

Observe

Why was Naaman more willing to do “some great thing” than to follow a simple instruction?

04

Look beneath the surface · 10 minutes

When success becomes worship

Success may be functioning as an idol when it controls our emotional stability, relationships, or view of ourselves.

These boxes are for private reflection. Nothing is submitted or saved.

05

The turning point · 10 minutes

Barter says “earn.”
Grace says “receive.”

The barter system

If I perform well, God and other people will owe me approval, protection, or prosperity.

The way of grace

Every good gift comes from God. His saving love is not a wage for our performance; it is received through Christ.

1 Corinthians 4:7What do you have that you did not receive?
James 1:17Every good and perfect gift is from above.

The greater servant

Jesus has already done the great thing.

The servant girl points Naaman toward healing despite the suffering caused by his nation. Jesus goes further: the true Suffering Servant bears the cost of forgiveness and gives us an identity we do not have to achieve.

06

Small groups · 15 minutes

Bring it into the light

  1. 01

    What lies beneath your desire to succeed or to be seen as successful?

  2. 02

    When does accomplishment stop being something you do and become the measure of your worth?

  3. 03

    Where do you slip into a barter relationship with God: “If I do this, God should do that”?

  4. 04

    What does Naaman need to surrender before he can receive grace?

  5. 05

    How does Jesus’ completed work free you to pursue excellence without being enslaved by it?

07

Response · 5 minutes

Lay down the need to prove yourself

Notice

Name one situation where performance is controlling your peace.

Receive

Thank God for one ability or opportunity as a gift rather than a possession.

Practice

Do one faithful act this week that may never be noticed or praised.

Closing prayer

Father, every good gift we have comes from you. Forgive us for turning achievement into our identity and treating your grace like a reward. Teach us to receive what Christ has done, to work faithfully, and to rest in your love. Amen.
